PROJECT CASE STUDY / BASKETBALL MODELING

Basketball Shot-Make Probability.

Estimating the probability of a made shot—not just predicting make or miss—using shot context, player movement, defensive pressure, and careful season-to-season validation.

01 / OBJECTIVE

A probability should mean something.

The modeling question

Given the observable context of a basketball shot at release, how accurately can a model estimate the probability that it goes in? A useful answer must distinguish an open attempt near the rim from a heavily defended long-range shot while avoiding unjustified confidence.

Why this is more than classification

The aim was to generate a probability between zero and one for every attempt. I therefore prioritized log-loss, calibration, and transfer across seasons instead of using classification accuracy alone to choose a model.

This was an independent technical modeling exercise using a supplied NBA shot-tracking dataset. It focused on feature design, repeatable evaluation, and a defensible final prediction pipeline.

02 / DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW

Build the evidence before choosing the model.

To make comparisons meaningful, I followed a progression from basic data checks to more flexible modeling approaches, keeping season-based validation central to the process.

  1. 01

    Audit

    Inspect types, missing values, identifiers, shot distributions, categorical levels, and potential leakage.

  2. 02

    Engineer

    Construct interpretable geometry, shot difficulty, defensive proximity, and movement features.

  3. 03

    Benchmark

    Compare a constant baseline, logistic models, GAMs, and restrained XGBoost candidates.

  4. 04

    Validate

    Evaluate held-out seasons, calibration, feature groups, and random-split sensitivity before final refitting.

PRIMARY VALIDATION

Season-held-out evaluation

Train on one season and assess on another, then reverse the comparison where feasible. This tests generalization across a time boundary rather than relying entirely on randomly mixed shots from both seasons.

≠
SENSITIVITY CHECK

Fixed random split

A conventional random split provides a comparison, but can look optimistic when the same players and environments appear in both sets. The season-based results carry more weight in model selection.

PRIMARY SCORING RULE

Log-loss

Log-loss penalizes confident incorrect probabilities. It lets a model be judged on the quality of its estimated chances, not merely whether the predicted class matches an outcome.

−[ y log(p) + (1 − y) log(1 − p) ]

Lower is better. The site intentionally does not display confidential model scores.

PROBABILITY QUALITY

Calibration

I compared predicted make probabilities with observed make rates across prediction bins and checked average prediction levels. A probability around 60% should correspond to approximately six makes in ten over many comparable attempts.

ROBUSTNESS

Feature-group ablations

Removing groups of inputs helped assess which information mattered to validation performance. Shot type and difficulty, along with defensive context, were important areas to investigate; smaller effects required careful interpretation.

I treated feature contribution as a diagnostic, not proof of a causal effect on shooting.

MODEL SELECTION

Controlled boosting

I compared restrained XGBoost configurations, chose boosting rounds from an internal holdout, and evaluated on separate season folds. I also examined whether combining GAM and boosting predictions added value.

The final workflow used the selected moderate XGBoost configuration rather than a more complex blend.

05 / TECHNICAL CHALLENGES

Make the modeling pipeline reproducible.

01

Trajectory and missingness

Challenge: Defensive approach measurements and contester fields require careful parsing and consistent treatment of missing entries.

Approach: Engineer release-time proximity and movement summaries while retaining meaningful missingness and avoiding ungrounded values.

02

Consistent features across seasons

Challenge: Categorical shot and possession states must map to the same model columns during training and testing.

Approach: Use aligned factor levels and sparse design matrices; check that train and test feature names and ordering agree.

03

Safe probability submission

Challenge: Scoring requires a valid probability for every unlabeled shot without silently changing row order or identifiers.

Approach: Refit the chosen model, check predictions are finite and strictly between zero and one, and validate that the output preserves the template's columns and shot ordering.

FINAL HANDOFF

A validated prediction pipeline, not just a fitted model.

The final stage selected boosting rounds using an internal holdout, refitted on all available training shots, generated probabilities for the testing set, and checked output structure. This illustrates the engineering and quality-assurance work surrounding model delivery.

06 / REFLECTIONS

What the project demonstrates—and what remains unknown.

Skills demonstrated

This work combined basketball-specific feature engineering, interpretable baselines, nonlinear predictive modeling, season-based validation, probability calibration, and rigorous output checks.

The central lesson was to favor a well-tested probability estimate over a model that merely appears accurate on a convenient random split.

Limitations and responsible interpretation

  • Generalization: Season holdouts are valuable, but cannot guarantee future performance under changing league and player conditions.
  • Available inputs: Some relevant shot mechanics and context may not be captured by the supplied tracking features.
  • Calibration: Reliable aggregate probabilities do not imply precise individual-shot certainty.
  • Scope: No claim is made about deployment by an NBA team or evaluation on a private external benchmark.

What I would explore next

With appropriate access and permission, I would test longer-term season drift, compare calibration methods on fresh held-out data, examine uncertainty in rare shot categories, and further investigate how defender movement changes predicted shot difficulty.
