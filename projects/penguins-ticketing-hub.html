01 / BUSINESS PROBLEM

Make ticketing questions measurable.

The opportunity

Sports business teams need to understand which events draw fans, how inventory converts to sales, which customer relationships persist across seasons, and where additional revenue might be possible. These questions often span game reports, ticketing systems, CRM data, and separate analysis tools.

What I built

I developed a multi-tab R Shiny business intelligence application around Pittsburgh Penguins home games. It combines real reported NHL attendance with a reproducible, fictional ticketing and customer database for an educational demonstration of revenue strategy and fan analytics.

The application offers interactive attendance trends, synthetic ticket revenue and sales channels, customer retention, a historical attendance backtest, and a customizable inventory-limited revenue scenario.

Important: This is an independent portfolio project, not an official Penguins product. No sales, ticket prices, customer records, membership figures, or financial values represent actual or estimated Penguins commercial performance.

02 / DATA ENGINEERING & GOVERNANCE

A hybrid dataset with a clear boundary.

Real attendance and fictional business records connect through verified game IDs, but they answer different questions and are never presented as interchangeable observations.

  1. 01

    Acquire NHL history

    Retrieve game schedules and NHL game-summary attendance from public sources; retain official game identifiers, opponents, venues, and source URLs.

  2. 02

    Verify arena scope

    Check game coverage and reported attendance. Exclude a designated Pittsburgh home game in Stockholm from PPG-specific analysis.

  3. 03

    Simulate ticket operations

    Create reproducible fictional customer, order, seat-category, channel, and membership records joined to the 122 PPG game IDs.

  4. 04

    Audit & publish

    Validate keys, totals, entitlements, model splits, and runtime inputs; prepare Shiny-ready aggregates and deploy the app.

Attendance is not ticket sales. The NHL's reported attendance does not disclose paid tickets, scanned entries, ticket prices, or revenue. The project's nominal 18,187-seat reference is not the game-by-game saleable inventory; a utilization ratio against it may sometimes exceed 100%.

MODEL SELECTION

A predeclared decision rule

I compared a historical-average baseline with ridge regression across a fixed penalty grid. To replace the simpler model, ridge had to improve validation mean absolute error (MAE) by at least 50 fans per game.

The best ridge candidate improved validation MAE by only 28.6, so the historical mean was selected before evaluating the final season.

Report what actually happened

On 40 held-out 2025–26 games, the selected historical mean recorded 1,172.6 MAE and 1,445.7 RMSE in attendees per game. Ridge had a lower holdout MAE of 1,128.6.

I did not change the selected model after viewing the holdout. This keeps the testing process honest instead of turning the final test into another tuning round.

05 / BUSINESS DECISION SCENARIO

Turn an assumption into a transparent what-if.

The scenario tool explores how converting a user-chosen share of remaining synthetic seats would change additional simulated ticket volume, gross revenue, and assumed marketing spend.

How the calculator works

For each simulated game, the model calculates remaining seats from synthetic sellable capacity and synthetic paid ticket volume. It then applies the assumed capture rate, rounds additional tickets down to whole seats, and multiplies by an assumed incremental net price.

Marketing spend is modeled separately as added tickets multiplied by the assumed acquisition cost per ticket. The remainder after that expense is not operating profit: it excludes other costs and potential displacement of existing sales.

Extra tickets per game = floor(unsold synthetic seats × assumed capture share)Illustrative gross revenue = extra tickets × assumed price

06 / DEVELOPMENT & QUALITY ASSURANCE

From pipeline to deployed business application.

01

Trustworthy data boundaries

Challenge: Actual attendance and invented commercial data can easily be mistaken for the same kind of observation.

Implementation: I labeled source origins throughout dashboards and exports, validated official attendance separately, and kept synthetic purchase and revenue variables out of the real-attendance model.

02

Correct revenue & retention math

Challenge: Membership payments span multiple game entitlements, while customer retention requires consistent full-season cohorts.

Implementation: The pipeline allocates fictional membership value once across games, derives weighted ticket KPIs, and distinguishes selected-game activity from complete seasonal renewal and retention denominators.

03

Reproducibility & cloud runtime

Challenge: R Shiny deployment needs the right prepared data and model artifacts, while large local datasets should not be committed to GitHub.

Implementation: Numbered build scripts, data dictionaries, independent audits, and a validated deployment bundle support a reproducible workflow without publishing simulated customer records or NHL report caches.

07 / RESULTS & REFLECTION

Business intelligence needs honest context.

What I delivered

A publicly deployed sports business analytics tool that integrates 122 verified venue games, a reproducible synthetic transaction and customer system, cohort-based retention analyses, and a documented historical attendance-model comparison.

The project demonstrates a full workflow: acquire and audit public data, create transparent business simulations, develop KPIs, test model decisions, and explain results to a nontechnical stakeholder.

What the results do not show

  • Real ticketing: Published NHL attendance is not an official count of tickets sold, scanned entries, or revenue.
  • Simulated operations: Ticket prices, membership renewal, fan segmentation, and channel performance are fictional.
  • Forecasting: The attendance model has only a retrospective 40-game final holdout, not a prospective deployment.
  • Scenarios: Incremental revenue assumes hypothetical buyers and does not prove promotional effectiveness.
  • Affiliation: This independent educational work is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Penguins, NHL, or arena.

How I would extend it

With authorized real ticketing access, the next steps would be more granular purchasing cohorts, sales-funnel conversion metrics, membership-renewal models, carefully evaluated demand forecasts, and pricing or marketing experiments with controlled outcome measurement.
