01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Predict a probability—not just a make or miss.

The question

Given the location, shot type, and game context available before a result is known, what is the probability an NBA field-goal attempt goes in? A good model should assign meaningful probabilities and retain their quality when evaluated on a later season.

What I built

I developed a public-data modeling workflow that compares a constant benchmark, logistic regression, a spatial generalized additive model, and a restrained XGBoost model. I then deployed the selected model inside a five-tab R Shiny application.

The main achievement is the combination of disciplined evaluation and practical communication: an interviewer can click a court location, set supported pre-shot context, review real historical shots, compare model scores, and inspect calibration and model explanations.

02 / DEVELOPMENT PIPELINE

A reproducible process from data to deployment.

Instead of training directly inside Shiny, I separated data collection, feature creation, model selection, evaluation, and app artifact preparation into auditable stages.

  1. 01

    Acquire & audit

    Collect NBA shot releases with hoopR; check shot outcomes, game identifiers, season coverage, court coordinates, and missing fields.

  2. 02

    Engineer

    Build rim distance, shot angle, court position, shot-type groups, action indicators, and pre-shot period/clock context.

  3. 03

    Compare models

    Evaluate a constant benchmark, logistic regression, spatial GAM, and regularized XGBoost across chronological folds.

  4. 04

    Freeze & validate

    Select the model before evaluating 2025–26; analyze log-loss, Brier score, calibration, and model explanations.

PROBABILITY QUALITY

Calibration, not just ranking

On the final 2025–26 holdout, the mean predicted make rate was 47.0% compared with 46.9% observed. This close aggregate agreement supports overall calibration but does not guarantee reliability for every rare shot context.

What these numbers mean: The selected XGBoost scored slightly better on the held-out season than during development (0.639 → 0.637 log-loss). This is evidence of stable observed generalization in this evaluation, not a promise of the same accuracy in future seasons or on unavailable defensive-tracking features.

06 / ENGINEERING & DELIVERY

A production-minded modeling workflow.

01

Pre-shot information only

Challenge: Outcomes, post-shot information, and identity shortcuts can inflate apparent predictive performance.

Approach: Use validated geometry, shot-type, and game-context features; exclude shooter and team IDs from the predictor set while retaining those IDs for exploratory filters.

02

Freeze the evaluation

Challenge: Repeatedly adjusting models after observing the last season undermines a credible final test.

Approach: Freeze modeling decisions with chronological folds, then evaluate the selected model on 2025–26 after the selection was locked.

03

Deliver a working application

Challenge: Model code alone does not create a responsive, useful analytical tool.

Approach: Save the frozen model and compact application artifacts, perform automated QA, manage dependencies with renv, and deploy Shiny without retraining at app startup.

07 / CONCLUSIONS & LIMITATIONS

Useful probabilities still need context.

What this project demonstrates

Public data acquisition, rigorous validation, advanced feature engineering, probabilistic model comparison, model interpretation, and delivery through a live interactive product.

The most important part is the workflow's integrity: selection criteria were applied before the final holdout, calibration was evaluated explicitly, and the trained result was made useful through Shiny rather than left in a notebook.

Important limitations

  • Public-data coverage: No defender tracking, shooter fatigue, release mechanics, or full optical movement data.
  • Population-level model: Team and player IDs are filters, not prediction features; the probability does not represent an individualized skill estimate.
  • Subgroup calibration: Very late-clock shots had the clearest identified calibration gap among larger evaluated subgroups (about 3.2 percentage points).
  • Future performance: One final holdout season cannot establish generalization to all future game contexts.
  • Interpretation: TreeSHAP explains model behavior, not a causal effect of any feature.

Relationship to my earlier basketball technical project

My earlier shot-make probability modeling case study documents a separate supplied-data technical exercise. This new project rebuilds the modeling approach using public NBA data and adds a reproducible, deployed interactive application. The two projects use different data and should not be treated as one continuous dataset or evaluation.
